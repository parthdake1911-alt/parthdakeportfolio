The short version
Problem

76% of proposed group trips never happen. Plans die in the chat because a “yes” costs nothing, and one friend ends up chasing everyone.

Insight

A trip isn’t real until people put money in. The missing step isn’t planning, it’s commitment.

Solution

Pay your share to say yes. Enough in by the date? The trip locks and the money goes to the organisers. If not, everyone is refunded.

Outcome

A working app, a brand system, a Figma file and a decision log. I cut 58 screens down to one clear flow along the way.

01 How I found the problem

I started by reading other people’s bad days.

I didn’t start with an app idea. I started with complaints. Travel apps in India have thousands of reviews, and one-star reviews are where people explain exactly what broke.

I collected 111 complaints from the Play Store (OYO, MakeMyTrip, Booking.com), Trustpilot, consumer complaint sites and Reddit, then read 780 recent OYO reviews one by one. I grouped them on an affinity board until patterns showed up.

Why plans fail (what I kept hearing)

  • 🗓️ Dates never line up, so the plan gets postponed forever.
  • 💸 Budgets go unspoken. People agree, then quietly drop out.
  • 🤷 “Maybe” costs nothing. Nobody actually commits.
  • 😮‍💨 One invisible organiser does everything and burns out chasing money.

Sources: practitioner write-ups (KahinToChalo), the WanderOn business model (fixed departures exist because friends’ plans fail), and my own friend groups.

04 Version 1 · the everything app

58 screens that didn’t make sense.

My first answer covered the whole trip, before, during and after: votes, budgets, stays, a live map, expenses, an album and a “Trip Wrapped” recap. Each idea was reasonable on its own. Together they told no story.

I ran a design crit on my own work

1

Money was asked too early. “What’s your budget?” can’t be answered before the place and travel exist.

2

Prices appeared from nowhere. “~₹8,600 each” depends on train or flight, hostel or villa.

3

The same question showed up in four places, and organiser and friend screens were mixed together.

4

Twelve budget modes. Powerful on paper, paralysing in practice.

Then a full UX audit

0issues found3 critical, 9 high, 9 medium, 10 low
0controls that did nothingDown to 14 after fixes
Worst finding: “Maybe” and “Can’t make it” were dead ends, and a friend who said yes landed on “Everyone’s in · 8 of 8” when only 6 were in. Usability went from about 5 to 7.5 out of 10 after the fixes, judged against Nielsen’s heuristics.

05 The honest pivot

“We are not even solving a problem.”

That was my own review of version 1, and it was right. The app was polished, but if you asked “what does it do?”, the answer took a paragraph. A good product answers in one line.

So I asked one question

What is the single moment where a friends’ trip lives or dies? It’s not choosing a place or splitting a bill. It’s the moment everyone has to actually commit.

The new one job

Friends pay their share to say yes. The trip locks when enough are in. If not, everyone is refunded.

What I cut, and what stayed

Place voteIn-app bookingPlan tabLive mapSwipe vote12 budget modes“See your friend’s phone”Trip Wrapped (for later) Pay to say yesLock at N peopleAuto refundsMoney always visible

The test for every feature became: does it help the group commit, pay fairly or remember? If not, it’s out.

06 The solution

Your money is your “yes”.

Paying a share is the clearest commitment there is, and it solves the organiser’s real pain: she no longer fronts money or chases replies.

Set the share

E.g. ₹5,000 each, usually enough for the stay. Pick the dates and how many must be in.

Friends pay to say yes

One WhatsApp link. The money is held by a licensed payment partner, not by the organiser or us.

Enough in? It locks

The money goes to the organisers to book anywhere. The home screen switches to “37 days to go”.

Not enough? Refunds

By the last date to join, everyone gets a full refund automatically. Nobody has to ask.

When things go wrong (designed, not ignored)

🏃

A friend drops out

Their seat is offered to the gang. Whoever takes it pays, and the dropout is refunded. Nobody else pays more.

🤒

The organiser can’t

“Hand over the trip”: money, bookings and nudges move to a new organiser. Anyone can be made an organiser.

🛑

Cancel everything

After the lock, one person can’t cancel alone. A majority must agree within 24 hours.

⏳

Not enough by the date

Automatic full refunds to the same UPI. Late joiners can still take a seat until it’s full.

07 Brand strategy

Paying your share is your ticket in.

I put on the brand strategist hat before drawing anything. Who is this for, what should it feel like, and what one object already means “I’m going”?

Positioning

For Indian friend groups who keep saying “this time for sure”, All In is the app where a yes means something, because you pay to say it.

Name

“All in” is what friends already say when they commit. It doubles as the action: going All In = paying your share.

Voice

Short, warm, a little cheeky. “Stop chasing. Lock the trip.” “0 reminders from Neha.” Never bank-speak.

08 Brand elements

A sticker box for every big moment.

A logo isn’t a brand. The brand is the stuff that shows up when something happens: a stamp when the trip locks, a ribbon when you settle up, a number that rolls instead of just changing. I designed these as a kit, each with one job and one way of moving. Tap any tile to replay it.

Objects
How things move
Drop0.6–0.8 s · overshoot

Cards and photos fall in at a slight angle, bounce once, and settle tilted.

Slam0.42 s · 2.4× → 1×

Stamps and pills hit hard, rotating from −30° to −12°.

Lock the trip.
Kinetic type70 ms per word

Big headlines rise word by word, like a story card.

Hover5.5 s · ±6 px

Once landed, floating cards breathe gently so screens never feel frozen.

Rule 1

One job each. The stamp means “done”, the pill means “paid”, confetti means “celebrate”. They never swap.

Rule 2

One sticker per card, never on top of text. My first perks pass was cluttered, so I cut it to the seal and the pill.

Rule 3

Motion is optional. With reduced motion on, everything appears in place, and no moment depends on animation to make sense.

09 UI problems I had to solve

The small things people actually notice.

Each of these came from testing the live app on a real phone or from feedback. Here is what broke and how I fixed it.

WelcomeBrand

“It looks AI.”

A sticker-and-odometer banner felt generated, not designed. I tried a photo banner, then rebuilt the screen in the pop style: four rotating scenes that each tell one step of the story (pay → lock → perks → settle), with the headline staying put.

Motion

“The animations look very normal.”

Fade-ins didn’t feel like a brand. I brought back the Wrapped-style motion kit: kinetic headlines, a ribbon, a LOCKED stamp that slams, odometer numbers, and kept it in every later version.

FormsBug

The calendar wouldn’t open

The phone’s native date picker doesn’t open inside some app frames, so “last date to join” was a dead tap. I designed a custom calendar sheet, then reused it for trip dates as a range picker (tap first day, tap last day), with quick picks like “New Year”.

Interaction

Ending a trip shouldn’t be a tap

Ending the trip changes the app for all 7 people, so a button was too easy to hit by accident. I used a swipe-to-end slider whose handle is the ticket. A short swipe springs back, and a full swipe opens the “That’s a wrap” moment. Keyboard users can press Enter.

CopyClarity

“6” of what?

The stepper said “Trip is on when · 6”, and nobody knew what 6 meant. It now reads “Trip locks when this many members have paid”, with “members” under the number and a live line: “Locks when 7 of 8 members have paid. Not by the last date? Everyone gets it back.”

Icon

An unreadable scooter

The travel icon read as a blob. I redrew it side-on, then removed its circle outline so it read at 20 px.

Layout

A logo on the headline

The new icon’s shine effect shared a class name with an old animation and jumped onto the headline. Renamed, and the icon moved next to the wordmark.

Spacing

A canyon above the button

On tall phones the spare space piled up above “Start a trip”. Now the banner absorbs it, and the button always sits 30 px under the text.

11 Reality check

Could this actually ship?

Every feature passed a feasibility check before it stayed in. Here is the money, the photos, the business, and every way a trip can go wrong.

Money

We never hold it

Shares sit with an RBI-licensed payment aggregator (escrow) until the trip locks. Refunds go back to the source. 18+ only, built for India’s DPDP rules.

Photos

About ₹1 per trip a month

Phones compress photos to ~0.5 MB and strip location. One trip ≈ 1,250 photos ≈ 0.6 GB on Cloudflare R2 ($0.015/GB-month, free egress). Signed links, trip members only.

Business

Free to plan, we earn when it happens

Gang perks: venues pay ~5% on bills paid through us, for quiet-slot group tables. Stay referrals at 2–6%. An optional organiser plan later.

A lesson from 2022: India’s restaurant association pushed back on deep dine-out discounts that venues paid for. In All In, the venue sets its own discount and hours, gets a guaranteed big table, and pays only on bills paid through us. Launch plan: Goa, about 30 venues.

13 Measuring it

What success looks like.

North Star

Locked trips: ideas that reach “enough are in” before the last date to join.

  • ⏱️ Time from creating a trip to locking it
  • 📣 Nudges the organiser sends herself (should go down)
  • 💸 Trips settled within 7 days of ending
  • 🔁 Groups that start a second trip

Usability test plan

Five people aged 21–28 who planned or joined a trip in the last year: three organisers and two “maybe” friends, on their own phones.

TaskPass bar
Create a Goa trip and share the linkUnder 1 min
From the link, pay your shareUnder 30 s
Explain what happens if not enough people payCorrect, in own words
End the trip and settle up3 taps or fewer

Success: 4 of 5 finish each task without help, and a SUS score of 75+. An India survey and five organiser interviews will replace the US statistics first.

What I learned

Cutting is design

The most impressive-looking features (travel pricing, a live map) were the ones making the product confusing.

One line or it’s not done

If I can’t say what the app does in one sentence, the user can’t either. “Pay your share to say yes” fixed every screen after it.

Design the failure paths

Drop-outs, refunds and hand-overs are what make people trust the happy path with real money.
