01 The problem

Three loved houses that didn’t feel like a family

Padaav runs three small properties: a kath-kuni mountain house in Himachal, an 1840s haveli in Jaipur and creek-side cottages on the Konkan coast. Guests who loved one rarely knew the others existed, and each house had its own look, its own booking page and its own emails.

The brief I set: make each house unmistakably of its place, make all three unmistakably Padaav, and run it all on one system that a small team can maintain.

02 Strategy

Distinct by place, connected by craft

Indian boutique groups usually pick an extreme: one look everywhere (strong recognition, flattened places) or every hotel on its own (local, but no family). I chose an endorsed brand architecture: each house leads with its own name and is always “a Padaav house”.

PadaavThe parent. Owns trust, booking, Passport and the halt dot.

The persona that justifies the whole system is the Collector: a guest who loved one house and wants the same care somewhere new.

So the parent brand earns its place with a product feature, not a logo: the Padaav Passport. Stay at all three houses and your next night is on us.

06 The design system

Build it once, re-skin it with one setting

Everything a brand can change lives in one Figma variable collection with four modes. Components never hold a raw colour, radius or font, and screens are built only from instances. Switch a frame’s mode and the whole screen becomes another house.

~45PrimitivesRaw colours, spacing, radii, durations. Hidden from designers.
23 × 4Brand modesColour, radius, motion, type style, brand copy and “is-house”.
35Components~120 variants, every property bound to a token, with usage notes.
304ScreensBuilt only from instances. No detached layers.
Button3 styles × 2 sizes × 5 states: default, hover, pressed, focus, disabled.
Room cardVertical for mobile, horizontal for desktop lists. Scarcity badge is optional.

10 Screens · when things go wrong

66 states for the moments that test trust

Loading, empty, error and the awkward in-betweens: a room sold while you were paying, a price that changed, a key that won’t work without Bluetooth. Each one says what happened and what to do next, and never relies on colour alone.

11 Screens · the staff side

Guests only see half the product. The staff console uses the same system in the Padaav master brand, with house filters. It answers the questions a front desk asks every morning: who’s arriving, which rooms are ready, and how the season is going.

13 A decision · photography

Real places need real photographs

The first version used AI-generated photos, and the whole product felt synthetic. I replaced every one with real photography and mapped each image to its own room, dish or experience, so Dhundh never shows a beach and Lehar never shows a haveli.

15 Process · how I used AI

AI built the volume. I made the calls.

I used Claude with the Figma MCP to research the market, draft strategy, generate token JSON and build components and screens programmatically in my Figma file. That’s how a 304-screen system fits in ten days. Every decision went through review, and a lot of AI output was changed or thrown away.

Day 1Strategy & nameMarket scan, positioning, 40 names → Padaav.
Days 2–3IdentityWordmark, halt dot, palettes, personality sliders.
Days 4–6Tokens & components4 brand modes, 35 components bound to variables.
Days 7–8ScreensGuest journey × 3 houses, states, console, emails.
Days 9–10QA & prototypeContrast audit, real photos, live prototype.
ChangedTypography. AI gave each house its own display face: six fonts in total. I consolidated to one family with a style per house.
RejectedAI photography. Generated photos made the product feel synthetic. Replaced with real photographs, each mapped to its room, dish or experience.
ChangedThe parent brand was invisible. House screens didn’t show Padaav. I added the family bar and the “a Padaav house” lock-up, driven by one boolean token.
ChangedColour. Rust-and-cream is the boutique cliché. I moved the master to ink-brown so the ochre dot is the only warm signature. Lehar’s teal failed contrast and was fixed.
KeptThe name. 40 options in five routes. “Padaav” stayed because the name itself explains the architecture: three stops on one journey.

The speed came from AI. The taste, the edits and the reasons are mine.

16 What I learned

Systems make restraint cheap

When every visual decision is a token, changing your mind costs minutes, not days. That made it easy to take risks early, like six typefaces and AI imagery, and pull them back once they didn’t work.

Test with travellersRun the booking flow with five real guests, starting with part-payment and cancellation.
Hand-draw the wordmarkThe halt dot deserves custom letterforms, not a typeset name.
Add “Journeys”Multi-house trips booked in one go: the natural next step for the Collector.
