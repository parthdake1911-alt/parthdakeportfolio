AMIN Worldwide member portal

I audited the member portal of AMIN Worldwide, a global network of independent agencies, and redesigned it around the members who use it. I designed the new experience as wireframes in Figma, then used Claude to build them into a working prototype you can click through.

My roleUX audit, UX/UI design, prototyping

ToolsFigma for wireframes; Claude to build the prototype

The problem

“The portal works, but it works for the platform, not the member.”

That was the headline of my audit. AMIN had plenty of content and a full community toolset. Members still struggled to find people, reach files and tell whether anyone was listening.

What was working

  • A rich content operation: 62 events, 441 threads and active staff curation
  • A full community toolset: peer groups, libraries and directories

Key problem areas

  • Finding people: 3 competing directories and search-only lookup
  • Reaching content: key files behind vague links and buried filters
  • Engagement signals: “0 replies” everywhere and hidden notifications

My takeaway

Fix findability and feedback loops first. Those two levers drive the metrics that matter to the network: connections made, event attendance and renewals.

01CriticalNavigation & information architecture

Members faced a maze before they could start any task

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • 10 menu items across two rows. An open menu covers the second row.
  • Insider jargon such as “AMIN Toolkit” and “KM Pillars”.
  • 20+ partner names crammed into a scrolling dropdown. Three “directories” (Who’s Who, Member, Company) give no clue how they differ, and one is a 74-page flipbook.
After My prototype
  • One row of seven task-based destinations: Dashboard, Members, Events, Library, Peer Groups, Opt-In Partners and Resources.
  • One place to find people. Members replaces the three overlapping directories.
  • Partners get a real page that shows what each partner does and what members get, with no dropdown hunting.
  • Plain-language labels: Library opens a “Knowledge Library”.
Audit observation

Members couldn’t find people or perks on their own, so they emailed staff. New members hit a wall of jargon before they got any value.

My recommendation

A one-row menu of 5–6 task-based groups, one “Find Members” destination and a proper Partner Benefits page.

Intended benefit

Every task starts from one predictable row, so members can self-serve instead of asking staff.

02HighDashboard experience

The home page showed activity but hid what mattered

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • A “25% complete” bar nags with no path to fix it.
  • Every post shows “0 replies”, so the community looks dead.
  • 11 notifications are hidden behind an illegible placeholder avatar.
After My prototype
  • An action strip leads the page: “Your week at a glance” shows events this month, unread discussions and connections.
  • Your next event is personal. A “Registered · Tomorrow” card shows the time in your zone, plus the Zoom link and details.
  • Profile progress has a path: the 60% ring links straight to a checklist through “Complete profile”.
  • “0 replies” becomes “Be the first to reply”, and notifications move to their own bell with a panel.
Audit observation

Zeros are negative social proof: they teach members that posting is pointless. Deadlines like the July 15 survey scrolled away and got missed.

My recommendation

Lead with an action strip of deadlines, registrations and profile tasks. Replace “0 replies” and give notifications their own bell.

Intended benefit

Members see what needs them first, and quiet threads read as an invitation rather than a dead end.

From my walkthrough: Instead of hunting for what matters, everything comes to you.

03CriticalMember directory

1,912 members, and the directory showed none of them

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • Blank exact-match fields, so you must already know who you’re looking for.
  • Search by email address, a value members by definition don’t have.
  • A greyed-out field with no explanation of why it’s disabled. There was no browsing, no expertise filters and no “people near you”.
After My prototype
  • Browse first. Members opens on an interactive globe of member agencies, listing the agencies in the region you’re viewing. You can open an agency to see its key people.
  • One search for people, agencies or locations, with suggestions to try.
  • A filterable grid of member cards: People, Agencies, AMIN Staff and Global Board, plus six regions. Each card shows role, agency and location, with a quick way to view the profile or get in touch.
Audit observation

Member-to-member connection is the product AMIN sells. A blank page that fails fuzzy searches makes the network feel smaller than it is.

My recommendation

A browse-first grid of member cards with one smart search box and filters for expertise, market and country.

Intended benefit

Members can discover people they don’t know yet, not only look up people they already do.

From my walkthrough: This is the heart of the redesign. Every member agency, visualized on a living map of the network.

04HighEvents experience

62 events, but finding the relevant one was manual labour

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • Filters are hidden behind a button, and hidden filters go unused.
  • Times appear in CT and SG with no conversion to the member’s time zone.
  • Recurring calls repeat every month, burying the unique events.
After My prototype
  • Filters sit inline: Upcoming and Past, plus event type (peer group calls, in person).
  • Times show in your time zone. Event pages spell out the other zones too, for example “9:00 AM CT · 4:00 PM CEST · 10:00 PM SGT”.
  • Recurring calls are tagged “Monthly series”, and “Registered” and “Tomorrow” badges show your status at a glance.
  • One-click Add to calendar, and a Cards/Calendar toggle.
Audit observation

Events are AMIN’s flagship programming. Manual scanning plus time-zone maths suppresses registrations and causes avoidable no-shows across a global network.

My recommendation

Default to the member’s peer groups, show local times, collapse recurring series, and add registration badges and one-click add-to-calendar.

Intended benefit

Members can find the right event, know when it really starts for them and commit in one step.

05CriticalDiscussion & community content

The most valuable content hid behind the word “HERE”

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • The webinar recording and deck can only be reached through a “HERE” link to an external folder.
  • The same resource appears as duplicate “related content” cards, and is posted three times in different places.
After My prototype
  • Files are hosted in the portal, not linked out. The Knowledge Library holds recordings, decks, templates and reports, and its search covers every file.
  • Each resource says what it is: type, length or size, and where it came from (for example, “from Q2 Member Webinar”).
  • One source of truth per event. The event page lists its recording, deck and data tables under “Event materials”.
Audit observation

Members couldn’t reliably self-serve files, so they emailed staff. External links break silently, escape search and are invisible to screen readers.

My recommendation

Host recordings and decks as native Library entries, with one source of truth per event, referenced everywhere else and never re-posted.

Intended benefit

Every file is findable from search, the Library or the event it belongs to, without a staff request.

From my walkthrough: All indexed in one searchable library, never buried behind links.

06MediumMember profile

Six identical “Add” boxes, so members added nothing

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • Six equal-weight empty sections with no guidance on what matters.
  • Unexplained badges: how are “Mentor” and “Mentee” earned?
  • Email shown publicly by default.
After My prototype
  • The completeness bar becomes a clickable checklist: “6 of 10 done, each step makes you easier to find”. Each open step has its own Add button.
  • The fields that power networking come first: expertise, markets, industries and languages. “Add the markets you serve” is labelled as powering directory matches.
  • Privacy by default: a “Review privacy settings” step notes that email is hidden by default.
Audit observation

Faced with six equal asks, members completed none; this profile sat at 25%. Empty profiles make everyone’s directory searches worse, a network-wide cost.

My recommendation

A short guided setup (photo, role, expertise, markets) with the long tail collapsed below, tied to the completeness bar as a clickable checklist.

Intended benefit

Members know which few details matter, and every completed profile makes the directory more useful for everyone.

From my walkthrough: A few details make you discoverable to the right partners across the network.

07HighAccessibility & consistency

Content locked in images, and controls too small to trust

Before Live portal, from my audit
  • 74 pages to page through, with no search and no links.
  • Tiny controls, far below a comfortable touch-target size.
  • Image-only pages, invisible to portal search and screen readers. Across the portal: white text on light gradients, links marked by colour alone, and three styles for the same buttons and cards.
In the prototype Partly addressed
  • The people in the flipbook become browsable: the member map, the member grid and global search now cover people and agencies. The Who’s Who booklet stays available as a download in the Library.
  • One component language: the same cards, chips and buttons are reused on every page of the prototype.

Not yet done

My audit recommends fixing the portal to WCAG AA: readable contrast, underlined links, 44px targets and one component style. I haven’t tested the prototype against WCAG AA yet. A contrast, target-size and screen-reader review is the next step before this could be built.

Audit observation

Accessibility gaps slow every member down, and create compliance exposure under the European Accessibility Act for a network with EU agencies.

My recommendation

Move the flipbook content into searchable profiles. Fix to WCAG AA: readable contrast, underlined links, 44px targets and one component style.

Intended benefit

Member information becomes searchable and usable by everyone, including people using screen readers.

+From my roadmap: search with scope and suggestions

One search across the whole network, and posting in one click

  • Global search opens as an overlay scoped across Members, Agencies, Events, Resources, Discussions and Communities, with example searches to try.
  • Results are grouped by type. Typing “margin” returns the Q2 webinar event, its recording, its deck and the benchmark data tables together.
  • “Get started” replaces a generic Create button, with three clear choices: a discussion thread, a Q&A thread or a library entry.

From my walkthrough: One search reaches across the entire network: people, events, resources, and discussions in seconds.

Reflection

Where this stands, and what I’d do next

Where it stands

This was a pitch project. The prototype shows the proposed experience. It hasn’t launched, and it hasn’t been usability-tested with AMIN members, so I make no claims about measured results.

What I’d do next

  • Test the one-row navigation with members, as my audit recommends
  • Add expertise and market filters to the member grid
  • Review the prototype against WCAG AA before any build
  • Track the outcomes my audit names: connections made, event attendance and renewals
